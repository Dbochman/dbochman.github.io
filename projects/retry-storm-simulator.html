    Scenarios

    The primary fails over and serves nothing for 15 s at 60% load. Clients already use jittered exponential backoff with 3 attempts.

    Not recovered by 120s: the trigger ended at 25s

    Observed: the backlog was still growing when the run ended, and 94.1% of the dependency's work went to requests whose caller had already given up. Each of those timeouts produced another retry, the self-sustaining loop called a metastable failure. Model bound: once every response arrives late, retries can hold demand at 3× normal (180% of capacity), so this model has no way to drain on its own.

    What to change

    • Cap retries as a share of traffic

      With a single retrying layer, a per-client budget (the SRE book uses 10% of requests) keeps sustained amplification near 1.1× however many attempts each request is allowed. Requests over the budget fail fast instead of joining the queue.

    • Stop serving callers who already left

      Propagate deadlines and drop queued work once its caller has timed out. Otherwise recovery capacity goes to responses nobody will read.

    • Backoff alone did not save it

      New requests keep arriving whatever the clients do, so backoff only delays retries. They still land on a saturated queue. Backoff helps with short blips and synchronized clients, but it does not cap load.

    • Or buy headroom

      In this model, the backlog always drains below 33.3% load with this policy. Above that it is fine until a trigger of about 1.5s or longer leaves it unrecovered at 120s. Treat both numbers as a direction for real capacity planning, not as thresholds.
